How this works
Tubing colour:
clean water,
cipro feed,
chlorine,
bisulfite. After mixing, the colour runs from orange (all cipro still readable by HPLC) to indigo (broken down), the same scale as the charts.
Flow in the coil:
800–3,000 water parcels each move at the laminar speed for their distance from the tube centre, jump sideways by diffusion (cipro, 7 × 10⁻¹⁰ m²/s, the model's estimate), and are carried around by the swirl the bend creates: two counter-rotating vortices (Dean flow, exact low-flow solution, Dean 1927). The inset shows those parcels in the tube's cross-section.
Chemistry:
the project's chemistry model (chlorine, chloramines, cipro, N-chloro-cipro and later products; Dodd 2005 rates) runs from the moment a parcel passes Tee 1. Each port reads the average over the parcels arriving there. Bisulfite removes all chlorine; N-chloro-cipro then keeps breaking down (or turns back into cipro, if Gap 2 is on) until discharge.
Start-up run:
every line starts full of clean water. Each stream advances at its real speed (pump flow ÷ tube cross-section). The run clock is real lab time; the animation plays it 10, 20 or 30 times faster.
Sizes:
jugs, pumps, controller and coil are drawn to real size. Connecting tubing uses the same inner diameter as the coil; the dechlorination line is sized to give the travel time you set and is wound on a 25 mm spool. The coil's height comes from its real number of turns at the tubing's outer diameter (inner diameter + 1.6 mm).
Limits: the swirl formula is exact for gentle swirl (Dean number under about 20; the page warns above that). The coil's pitch and the slight change in the main flow profile from bending are ignored. Mixing at each tee is treated as instant, and each parcel is its own small beaker. Chlorine lost to tubing walls and temperature are not modelled. Pump tubing inside each pump head is not counted in line lengths. The November dye test measures the real spread.
