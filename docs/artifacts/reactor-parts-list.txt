Coil:
wind the tube tightly on the PVC pipe, turns touching, and hold it with zip ties. 1/4″ OD PTFE kinks below about a 44 mm bend radius, so don't use anything smaller than the 4″ pipe.
Ports A–C:
cut the tube at each distance, push both ends into a union tee, and put a shut-off valve on the side branch. Draw samples from the valve with a syringe.
Pump connections (4):
PTFE tube → John Guest female adapter → 1/16″ barb (wrap the thread in PTFE tape) → the pump's own 2 mm BPT tube.
Probes:
handheld pens, no logging. Check the buffered feed jug with the pH pen at the start and end of each run and spot-check a Port A sample; use the ORP pen to confirm quenched vials have no chlorine left.
Quench by hand:
make 0.30 g/L sodium bisulfite fresh each run day; put 100 µL in each labelled 2 mL vial (double it for 10 mg/L runs). Run 0.5 mL to waste from the port, collect 2 mL, squirt it into the vial, cap, shake, note the time. For plant-timing samples hold 5 min before filtering. Check one vial per run day reads 0.00 mg/L on the DPD kit. The outlet still contains chlorine: collect it in a waste jug with a spoonful of bisulfite.
Chlorine tests
take 10 mL each, which is 10 minutes of flow at 1 mL/min. Collect the sample over that time, or check chlorine in a separate run at higher flow.
Check before you order:
Amazon blocks automated reading of product pages, so these items were picked from their listing titles and prices are estimates. Confirm three things: the pump listing is the 24 V stepper version with 2 mm × 4 mm BPT tubing; the 1/16″ barb fits snugly in 2 mm ID tubing; and each John Guest fitting says 1/4″ OD.
