Coil:
wind the tube tightly on the PVC pipe, turns touching, and hold it with zip ties. 1/4″ OD PTFE kinks below about a 44 mm bend radius, so don't use anything smaller than the 4″ pipe.
Ports A–C:
cut the tube at each distance, push both ends into a union tee, and put a shut-off valve on the side branch. Draw samples from the valve with a syringe.
Pump connections (4):
PTFE tube → John Guest female adapter → 1/16″ barb (wrap the thread in PTFE tape) → the pump's own 2 mm BPT tube.
Probes:
handheld pens, no logging. Check the buffered feed jug with the pH pen at the start and end of each run and spot-check a Port A sample; use the ORP pen to confirm quenched vials have no chlorine left.
Quench by hand (plan v2):
make 0.30 g/L sodium bisulfite fresh each run day; put 150 µL in each labelled 4 mL amber vial. Run 0.5 mL to waste from the port, collect 3.0 mL, squirt it into the vial, cap, shake, note the time. That adds about twice the bisulfite needed at 5 mg/L and dilutes the sample × 1.05. Hold for the time Experiment 4B sets, then filter into a 2 mL HPLC vial. Check one vial per run day reads 0.00 mg/L on the DPD kit. The outlet still contains chlorine: collect it in a waste jug with a spoonful of bisulfite.
Chlorine tests
take 10 mL each, which is 10 minutes of flow at 1 mL/min. Collect the sample over that time, or check chlorine in a separate run at higher flow.
Check before you order:
Amazon blocks automated reading of product pages, so these items were picked from their listing titles and prices are estimates. Confirm three things: the pump listing is the 24 V stepper version with 2 mm × 4 mm BPT tubing; the 1/16″ barb fits snugly in 2 mm ID tubing; and each John Guest fitting says 1/4″ OD.
Decide before you order: the static mixer.
Plan v2 calls for an HPLC-type inline mixer (about 50–150 µL) after the chlorine tee, but the ones found cost far more than anything else on this list: a complete 50 µL PEEK assembly is
$759.50, rated for only 20–150 µL/min
, below this coil's 1 mL/min, and a 150 µL stainless cartridge is
$512
before its housing. Both use 1/16″ fittings, so they also need adapters to the 1/4″ push-fit line. Cheap luer-lock mixers (about $1–8) were found too, but no hold-up volume is listed, and the ones with acetal elements may not suit chlorinated water. Ask BioCurious about donated parts on the tour. No mixer cost is in the totals above.

Pumps and controller
Two pumps: feed and chlorine. At 1 mL/min a 50 mL/min pump runs at about 2% of full speed. A stepper motor handles that well; calibrate each pump by weighing its output (1 g = 1 mL): 10 minutes for the feed pump, 30 minutes for the chlorine pump, as in
controller/README.md
. An Arduino makes the step pulses; your laptop runs
pumpctl
over USB (calibration, run timer, logs). If the laptop goes quiet for 5 s, the Arduino stops both pumps by itself.
