## 1. Read this before filling in the form

**Three things block the application as it stands.** None of them are budget problems.

### 1.1 An individual cannot be the applicant

Valley Water's Mini-Grant page states that individuals cannot apply directly. An organised community group can, provided it has "an established structure" and names an individual responsible for signing. Schools and colleges are separately listed as eligible.

So the applicant of record has to be one of:

- **MVHS or FUHSD** — the cleanest route, and it makes lab space, a supervising teacher and school equipment legitimate in-kind contributions rather than assumptions.
- **A school club with officers and a faculty advisor** — may satisfy "established structure." Confirm with the grants team.
- **A watershed or creek non-profit** that agrees to sponsor the project.

### 1.2 The published figures disagree with each other

| | Mini-Grant landing page | Mini-Grant detail page |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum award | $10,000 | **$5,000** |
| Match required | None | **At least 25 % of total project cost, monetary or in-kind** |

The budget below is built to the **conservative reading** — a request under $5,000 with match well above 25 % — so it is valid either way. Confirm the real figures with the grants team before submitting.

### 1.3 The project needs a public-benefit component

Mini-Grant projects must deliver public benefit under water conservation, flood protection or environmental stewardship, and must carry Valley Water's logo on project materials. A school-lab project delivers none of that as scoped, so the budget keeps a real outreach task: two public workshops (now demonstrating the blue-to-pink bacteria test and the model), a handling demonstration unit, and an open release of the model code, device design, test protocol and dataset. Outreach costs about 50 person-hours; decide whether to run it before or after the March 2027 fair.

**Framing for the application (new):** the project gives utilities a way to check that treated water no longer carries antibacterial activity, and uses the model to cut chemical dosing. Local tie: Valley Water's purified-water program and its RO concentrate discharges to the Lower South Bay.

## 6. The one-project-per-programme warning

The form says projects that are part of another Valley Water programme are ineligible. Nothing in the plan touches Adopt-A-Creek, sponsorships or rebates. If Valley Water staff donate lab time or a water sample, disclose it in the application and ask the grants contact first.
